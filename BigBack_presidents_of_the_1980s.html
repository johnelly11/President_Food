What Was on the Menu For the big Jimmy Carter and the big Ronald Reagan

From cheesy grits to a big ol' yummy bowl of hamburger soup, what did the candidates like to eat?

By: THE Greg Nelson · Food & stuff

When it comes to presidents and their favorite foods, the White House can fulfill any of their big back needs. From an interview conducted by me a local high school student named Trey Harris said "Hamburger soup sounds kinda good, WAIT OOH I love cheesy grits."

Carter, a peanut farmer from Plains, Georgia, of course enjoyed southern food. He also really liked peanuts, obviously he's a peanut farmer, but one food stood out from the rest. Cheesy Grits was known to be Jimmy Carter's favorite food. Cheesy Grits are a southern mix of ground corn porridge, heavy cream, and a lot of sharp cheddar. (That actually sounds so good,)

Reagan, by contrast, was known for plain American comfort food. (my kinda guy) Reagan's favorite food was hamburger soup, a scrumptious mix of hamburger and vegatables in a tomato broth. (this one is so delicious) One unpopular opinion from Ronald Reagan is that he LOVED black licorice jelly beans.

Carter: A Taste of The South

Carter's food tastes show his roots. Grits and peanuts made a regular appearance at his breakfast table, and his background as a peanut farmer made the peanut a lasting part of his public image. Other southern staples also fit into his palatte, but most prominentally cheesy grits

Reagan: Yummy Comfort Food

Hamburger soup is a thick, savory dish usually made with ground beef, tomatoes, and mixed vegetables. Its appeal is simplicity: cheap ingredients, one pot, and plenty of leftovers. For Reagan, it represented the kind of straightforward home cooking many Americans grew up with. (Thats my president)
